`ritmo-tools rig`: characters animated on a skeleton, as code. A bank (`hero.rig.json`) names a rig module (bones in a tree with a setup pose, vector parts drawn on them anti-aliased, two-bone IK chains, props, and named stances) and a few drafts of each clip, each an ES module whose `pose(rig, t, p)` poses the rig on every frame. Clip modules may import the rig module and each other for shared stances and constants.

- A pose turns bones (`bones`), puts a chain's end on a goal (`reach`: a world point, or a point on another bone moved in world axes, like a fist in front of the shoulder), and sets a bone's world angle (`aim`: "the blade at 245°", "the thigh at -40°, the shin hanging"); on each bone an aim wins over a reach and a reach over a turn, each by its `mix`. `rig.keys` is the one keyframe helper (curves, `rest` keys for slow-in and slow-out). Every channel starts on frame 0 from the clip's `stance` (or the setup pose), so a clip is its stance until its keys change it; an aim or reach the stance lacks mixes in from 0. It refuses key lists that would key the wrong motion, naming the clip, frame, and channel: two keys on one frame for one channel, a key past the clip, and turns an aim or reach always overrules.
- A prop (`props: { sword: [...parts] }`) is shown, swapped, or hidden as one (`props: { sword: null }`); a hidden prop's bones are exempt from every check and from the trail.
- `rig check` poses every draft twice and lists errors (a module that does not load, a pose naming what the rig does not have, keys mistakes, randomness not from the seed) and warnings, each with its frames and bones or parts: off the canvas, under the ground, a planted foot that floats, sinks, or slides against the clip's travel, a goal out of reach, a far-side part peeking behind the back, a joint locked or hyperextended, a sudden change of speed outside `fast` phases (not on the first and last frames of a move out of or into rest), a loop that stops or jumps, a frame with nothing drawn, a draw-order change, and a first or last frame off the clip's `stance`. It reports each joint's bend, the trail bone's spacing, peak speed, and arc, a verdict per draft and per clip, and with `--positions` the world place of the bones named on every frame. A draft's `accept` records a warning judged deliberate, with why.
- `rig look` writes contact sheets no wider than 2000 pixels (each frame numbered with its phase, the bones under each row; `--frames a-b`, `--art`, `--each`, `--onion`, `--scale`), with the person's comments ringed. `rig bake` writes the clip in use as a sprite sheet PNG, its JSON (Pixi, Phaser, Aseprite, with `meta.loop`), and a GIF; the same inputs give the same bytes. `rig notes` and `rig resolve` read and close the person's comments from the review file next to the bank (`hero.rig.review.json`), pinned to a pixel of a frame or to a span of frames. `rig init` writes a starting bank.
- `rig bake` also writes one versioned sidecar per bank (`hero.rig.json` → `hero.rig.clips.json`, every clip) for gameplay: each clip's draft, timing, travel, stance, and phases, and on each frame its name in the atlas, the feet on the ground, the rig's and clip's named `sockets` (position and angle), the `hit` capsule (a segment and its radius; the trail bone on `fast` frames by default, or a clip's `hit: { bone, from, to, radius, frames }`, or `hit: false`), and the drawn box, in pixels from the anchor with y down. The file is always complete: `bake <names>` rewrites only those clips' entries and keeps the others. The game reads it with its own Standard Schema; the atlas JSON stays the hash format Pixi and Phaser load.
- Image parts: a part's shape can be a picture (`{ kind: "image", file, pivot, at, angle, scale }`), its pivot pixel on a point of its bone, turned and scaled with the bone, sampled bilinearly and composited premultiplied; shapes and pictures mix freely, props and slots work on both, and a part's `tint` darkens a far side.
- `rig parts <sheet.png> [--names ...]` cuts an AI-made character parts sheet into one transparent PNG per part (the pixel fixer's background clearing; the parts keep their anti-aliased pixels) and `parts/parts.json` with each part's box, long axis, and a guessed pivot (the point on its long axis nearest the sheet's centre of mass, half its width in from an end; the torso's lower end), warning about labels and specks. `rig init --from parts.json` scaffolds a side-view humanoid rig from those pictures, with plain shapes for missing parts and other parts as props, and an idle, so the first look shows it standing.
- `rig look --compare` writes one picture of every draft of a clip, a row each, the same moments in each column.
