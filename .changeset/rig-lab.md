`ritmo-tools rig`: characters animated on a skeleton, as code. A bank (`hero.rig.json`) names a rig module (bones in a tree with a setup pose, parts drawn on them as anti-aliased shapes or pictures, two-bone IK chains, props, and named stances) and a few drafts of each clip, each an ES module whose `pose(rig, t, p)` poses the rig on every frame. Clip modules may import the rig module and each other for shared stances and constants. The review page also runs on the website with the example character, Borri, a dwarf rigged from a painted parts sheet: https://ritmo.typeonce.dev/tools/rig/.

- A pose turns bones (`bones`), puts a chain's end on a goal (`reach`: a world point, or a point on another bone moved in world axes, like a fist in front of the shoulder), and sets a bone's world angle (`aim`: "the blade at 245°", "the thigh at -40°, the shin hanging"); on each bone an aim wins over a reach and a reach over a turn, each by its `mix`. `rig.keys` is the one keyframe helper (curves, `rest` keys for slow-in and slow-out). Every channel starts on frame 0 from the clip's `stance` (or the setup pose), so a clip is its stance until its keys change it; an aim or reach the stance lacks mixes in from 0. In a loop every channel wraps instead: after its last key it moves on to its frame-0 value at `frames`, smooth across the seam, so a loop keys only what changes. A key's `curve` shapes the segment it starts. It refuses key lists that would key the wrong motion, naming the clip, frame, and channel: two keys on one frame for one channel, a key past the clip (or, in a loop, on frame `frames`, which is frame 0 again), and turns an aim or reach always overrules. `rig.add` lays motion written as a function of `t` (a wave) over a pose.
- A prop (`props: { sword: [...parts] }`) is shown, swapped, or hidden as one (`props: { sword: null }`); a hidden prop's bones are exempt from every check and from the trail.
- `rig check` poses every draft twice and lists errors (a module that does not load, a pose naming what the rig does not have, keys mistakes, randomness not from the seed) and warnings, each with its frames and bones or parts: off the canvas, under the ground, a planted foot that floats, sinks, or slides against the clip's travel, a goal out of reach, a far-side part peeking behind the back, a joint locked or hyperextended, a sudden change of speed outside `fast` phases (not on the first and last frames of a move out of or into rest), a loop that stops or jumps, a frame with nothing drawn, a draw-order change, and a first or last frame off the clip's `stance`. It reports each joint's bend, the trail bone's spacing, peak speed, and arc, the hit's travel on each frame it hits on (which way its far end goes, and how far its bone turns), a verdict per draft and per clip, and with `--positions` the world place of the bones named on every frame. A draft's `accept` records a warning judged deliberate, with why.
- `rig look` writes contact sheets cropped to the character and the ground line (`--full` for the whole frame), the frames in one row when they fit in 2000 pixels and in even rows when not (each frame numbered with its phase, the bones under each row; `--frames a-b`, `--art`, `--each`, `--onion`, `--scale`, fractional too), with the person's open comments ringed where their part is now; `--compare` writes one picture of every draft of a clip, a row each, the same moments in each column. `rig notes` and `rig resolve` read and close the person's comments from the review file next to the bank (`hero.rig.review.json`), pinned to a spot of a frame or to a span of frames. `rig init` writes a starting bank.
- `rig bake` writes the clip in use as a sprite sheet PNG, its JSON (Pixi, Phaser, Aseprite, with `meta.loop`), and a GIF, and one versioned sidecar per bank (`hero.rig.json` → `hero.rig.clips.json`, every clip) for gameplay: each clip's draft, timing, travel, stance, and phases, and on each frame its name in the atlas, the feet on the ground, the rig's and clip's named `sockets` (position and angle), the `hit` capsule (a segment and its radius; the trail bone on `fast` frames by default, or a clip's `hit: { bone, from, to, radius, frames }`, or `hit: false`), and the drawn box, in pixels from the anchor with y down. The sidecar is always complete: `bake <names>` rewrites only those clips' entries and keeps the others. The game reads it with its own Standard Schema. `--scale` takes any number above 0 up to 8 (0.5 bakes a rig drawn at a sheet's size at half of it). The same inputs give the same bytes.
- Pictures as parts: a part's shape can be a picture (`{ kind: "image", file, pivot, at, angle, scale }`), its pivot pixel on a point of its bone, turned and scaled with the bone, sampled bilinearly and composited premultiplied; shapes and pictures mix freely, props and slots work on both, and a part's `tint` darkens a far side. `rig parts <sheet.png> [--names ...]` cuts an AI-made character parts sheet into one transparent PNG per part (the pixel fixer's background clearing; the parts keep their anti-aliased pixels) and `parts/parts.json` with each part's box, long axis, and a guessed pivot, warning about labels and specks. `rig init --from parts.json` scaffolds a side-view humanoid rig from those pictures, each bone's direction from its role and each picture kept as drawn when it was drawn upright or flat (the head, torso, and feet always), with plain shapes for missing parts, any other part a prop on a bone of its own on the torso (before the arms, so either hand can reach it, the near one in the stance), and an idle keyed as a loop, so the first look shows it standing; the module owns the pivots from then on. `examples/rig/borri` is a rig an agent made this way from `examples/rig/sheet/character.png`, a painted sheet a script makes.
- `rig review <bank>` opens a local review page in the effects lab's look: every draft of a clip on one clock, a frame strip with fast frames, key frames, and span bands, and the clip's Review, Comments, Tune, Check (with Show), and Toggles (bones, onion skin, ground line). A click pins a comment with the part there, its own bone, and the spot in that bone's frame, so its ring follows the part after an edit; S marks a span. With the bones on, dragging a picture part's pivot saves it to the bank's `pivots`, laid over the rig module's on load and listed by `rig notes` for the agent to fold in. The server watches the bank's folder and rebakes what changed, reading the modules and their imports anew, and the page swaps the frames in. With `--host` (a phone) the frames come at 1×. The review file is version 2 (a pixel comment's spot on its bone); version 1 files read as they are.
